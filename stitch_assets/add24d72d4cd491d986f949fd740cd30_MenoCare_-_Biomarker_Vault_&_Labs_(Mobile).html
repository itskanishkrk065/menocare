Endocrine Reference Guides

Why FSH alone does not diagnose perimenopause expand_more

In STRAW Stage -1, the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis experiences pulsatile surges. FSH can measure 40 mIU/mL on Monday and fall to 12 mIU/mL by Thursday as an irregular cohort of follicles suddenly recruits.

Clinical diagnosis relies on longitudinal symptom patterns alongside cycle length variability (>7 day persistent shift) rather than a single static blood draw.

The role of progesterone in neuro-calm expand_more

Progesterone naturally metabolizes into allopregnanolone, a potent positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A neuroreceptors in the limbic system.

During perimenopause, as anovulatory cycles become frequent, progesterone is the first hormone to precipitously drop, frequently precipitating nocturnal wakefulness and heightened somatic anxiety.
